## RadLab and the September 2017 event

- NASA OSDR's RadLab identifies `MSL-RAD-Surface` as the Curiosity rover's Mars surface instrument, with an advertised data cadence of 17 minutes. The API documents `absorbed_dose_rate` in µGy/hour and separately lists `dose_equivalent_rate` in µSv/hour. Credit NASA OSDR RadLab, record the actual portal version and retrieval date, and name Curiosity's Radiation Assessment Detector. The published release history lists 1.1.2 as its latest documented release. [Instrument](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/gui/knowledgebase/MSL-RAD-Surface) · [API fields](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/gui/data-api) · [OSDR acknowledgment guidance](https://science.nasa.gov/reference/osdr-faq/) · [Release history](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/gui/knowledgebase/Release_history)
- The timestamp knowledgebase gives an ISO-style representation and Unix filter support. An exact single-row API cross-check using ISO `2017-09-11T08:01:51` and its UTC Unix equivalent `1505116911` returned the same offset-free timestamp, instrument ID, and value of 15.384445 µGy/hour. This verifies the UTC interpretation operationally for the checked row. The fixture retains original strings and applies UTC explicitly for archive-to-sol mapping. [Timestamp documentation](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/gui/knowledgebase/timestamp) · [ISO probe](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/api/?instrument=MSL-RAD-Surface&timestamp=2017-09-11T08%3A01%3A51&absorbed_dose_rate&format=csv) · [Unix probe](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/api/?instrument=MSL-RAD-Surface&timestamp=1505116911&absorbed_dose_rate&format=csv)
- The full query retrieved on 12 November 2026 contains 1,220 original readings from 4 September 00:00:57 to 17 September 23:54:35 UTC. The chart covers 330 populated hourly bins and leaves six missing hours blank. The longest reading-to-reading gap is 24,276 seconds on 10 September. The raw CSV and derived manifest can be checked offline with `node scripts/nasa/import-radlab.mjs --verify`. [Original API query](https://visualization.osdr.nasa.gov/radlab/api/?instrument=MSL-RAD-Surface&timestamp%3E=2017-09-04&timestamp%3C2017-09-18&absorbed_dose_rate&format=csv)
- NASA JPL and RAD investigators tie the September 2017 Mars surface rise to a solar energetic particle event observed by Curiosity. The chart can describe the measured rover radiation environment and the real event. A replay alignment with fictional crew events is contextual and cannot establish crew dose, habitat shielding, clinical effects, or causation. JPL's PDS documentation gives the mean Mars solar day as 88,775.245 Earth seconds, which rounds to the contract's 88,775 seconds. [NASA JPL event report](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/large-solar-storm-sparks-global-aurora-and-doubles-radiation-levels-on-the-martian-surface/) · [Ehresmann et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018GL077801) · [JPL PDS time convention](https://planetarydata.jpl.nasa.gov/img/data/mer1-m-pancam-5-mesh-ops-v1.0/mer1mw_0xxx/document/mer_pppcs_excerpts.pdf)

## Health monitoring and escalation

- NASA-STD-3001 Volume 1 identifies the Crew Medical Officer and Flight Surgeon roles (§3.1, V1 3000). It calls for in-mission physiological and psychosocial monitoring including sleep (§3.4, V1 3003), and tracking vital signs and behavioral health signs (§3.5, V1 3004). It recognizes stored-and-forward private medical communications when delay prevents real-time contact (§6.2.1, V1 6002). NASA's Earth-Independent Medical Operations concept supports the broader idea of crew autonomy. These sources support the workflow and role labels. The prototype's baseline parameters, finding thresholds, routing rules, and mood scale need their own review status and carry no clinical validation from those sources. [Crew health standard, section 3](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/3-0-health-and-medical-care/) · [Medical operations, section 6](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-medical-operations/) · [EIMO concept](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240001402)
- NASA describes Mars communication latency as up to 20 minutes one way. At the prototype's simulated 20 minutes each way, the earliest signal round trip is 40 minutes before review or handling time. [NASA distance from Earth hazard](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-distance-from-earth/)
- General measurement references cover seated upper-arm blood pressure, wrist pulse, thermometer use and consistent method, glucose meter handling, and sleep logging. The operational equipment and procedure would be mission-defined. Treat the 1–5 mood entry as a simple self-report scale defined by the demo. [American Heart Association blood pressure guide](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings/monitoring-your-blood-pressure-at-home) · [MedlinePlus pulse](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003399.htm) · [MedlinePlus temperature](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003400.htm) · [FDA glucose meter guidance](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/how-safely-use-glucose-meters-and-test-strips-diabetes) · [NHLBI sleep diary](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/resources/sleep-diary)
